# n28-f20-barrier-alu — native prediction and falsification criteria

No GPU was used. Everything here is a prediction with an explicit test; nothing is claimed as
landed.

Identity to capture: ord 230, `s37_fold_body_r1a_v1`, kernel hash of the parent
`76902ef344609c8a`, plan row `PASS 151 … 32 29184 6 32 169 97 1`, push p0 = (336, −1, 0, 0).

| 3 | `store.ugm.d32.a32.bss[a0.0]` + `[A+0x4/0x8/0xC]` in the tail | 4 | **1** | the tail store was not re-owned → class A |

instructions while six warps have retired; the candidate ends with eight warps of ~130
instructions. Wall-clock tail ≈ max(issue 8×130, latency 32 dependent mul→add steps) instead of
max(issue 2×500, latency 32 steps with 4-way ILP).

## 2. Counter prediction (the card's acceptance)

Ord 230's measured profile on the chain head (`evidence/STALL-BUDGET.json`): gpu 1.346 ms,
idle 0.362 ms, **BARRIER 0.224 ms**, **ALUWR 0.292 ms**, SENDWR 0.029 ms.

* `XVE_STALL_BARRIER`: **down**. One of the five executed barriers disappears, and the one that
  is removed is the one immediately before the least balanced work in the kernel (two subgroups
  doing 64 rows). Direction is unambiguous; magnitude is bounded by 0.224 ms × (share of that
  barrier) and cannot be predicted better without a per-barrier capture, which the toolchain
  does not give us.
* `XVE_STALL_ALUWR`: **down in the tail, flat elsewhere**. The tail's per-lane dependency chain
  is unchanged in length (32 fp32 adds, contract §3 freezes the order) but the number of chains
  per lane falls 4 → 1 and the warps executing it rise 2 → 8, so the ALU write-back pressure
  that the 4-channel form created in two warps is spread over eight. Risk: with one chain per
  lane the intra-lane ILP is 1 instead of 4; if the XVE has enough resident warps (5 workgroups
  × 8 warps) that is hidden, if it does not, ALUWR could rise — that is exactly what the
  two-arm counter comparison will show.
* `XVE_STALL_SENDWR`: **flat** (0.029 ms; four messages per workgroup removed, which is noise).
* `GpuTime` for the PASS: expected slightly lower; the frame-level delta is expected to be
  < 0.03 ms, i.e. below the L13 floor for a single point, so ordinary must be read together with
  the counter direction, not alone.

## 3. What would falsify the performance claim

1. `sync.bar` 12 → 11 and the tail ops as in §1, but `XVE_STALL_BARRIER` **up**: then the
   removed barrier was not a stall source and the wait simply moved into the pipeline — the
   mechanism is real but not worth anything (class C).
2. `sync.bar` 12 → 11, tail instruction count down, `ALUWR` **up** and the frame slower: the
   4-way ILP inside a lane was doing real work in hiding the FP32 latency, and eight
   single-chain warps are worse. Then the follow-up is to keep the owner alignment (the barrier
   removal) but restore two channels per lane (64 rows × 2 halves = 128 slots… i.e. 8 lanes per
   row at 2 channels each, which needs 64 rows × 2 = 128 lane-slots = 4 warps, not 8) — that is
   a different candidate, not a re-run of this one.
3. Tail store/load counts unchanged: implementation did not land (class A), not a mechanism
   verdict.

## 4. What is *not* claimed

* Not claimed that the frame gets faster; that needs the two-arm ordinary measurement.
* Not claimed that `XVE_STALL_BARRIER` falls by a specific number of milliseconds.
* Not claimed that the four remaining barriers are removable; `BARRIER-AUDIT.md` §2 shows the
  cross-subgroup read/write sets that keep them.
* Not claimed that the ALU address chains were shortened — they were audited and left alone
  (`RESUME.txt` §"ALU chain audit" records why).
