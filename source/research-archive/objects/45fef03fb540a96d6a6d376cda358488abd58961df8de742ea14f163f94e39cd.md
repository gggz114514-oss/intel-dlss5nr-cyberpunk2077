### 2.1 The constant that matters: **8 SLM messages per fragment transfer**

Source-level cooperative-matrix tile traffic (all loops unrolled, exactly as IGC
emits them) versus the ISA counts:

| stage | `coopMatLoad` from shared | `coopMatStore` to shared | messages |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 expand (M) | 8 (A from `shX`) | 8 (M into `shArena`) | 128 |
| 3 contract (Y) | 10 (A ×8 + 2 seeds) | 2 (Y into `shY`) | 96 |
| 4 QKV | 0 (A is registers) | 6 (Q/K/V into `shArena`) | 48 |
| 6 score | 10 (Q ×2 + K^T ×8) | 0 | 80 |
| 8 context | 8 (V) | 2 (debug `NR_CTX`) | 80 |
| 9 projection (O) | 2 (seed `shY`) | 2 (O into `shX`) | 32 |
| **total** | **38** | **20** | **464** |

`304 = 38 × 8` and `160 = 20 × 8` **exactly**, and the per-stage blocks match
independently (`seg2` 64/64, `seg3` 16, `seg5` 48, `seg7` 16, `seg10` 16).  So on
this target **one 8×16 / 16×16 fragment transfer costs 8 SLM messages** — 8×
more slots than a weight B fragment costs in global memory (1 `load.ugm.d32x8`).
That asymmetry is the whole cost structure of F20: 464 of 640 runtime messages
(72.5 %) exist to move cooperative-matrix tiles through SLM.

### 2.2 The staging tax, itemised per fragment transfer

| item | measured | note |
|---|---|---|
| SLM messages | 8 | §2.1 |
| register-slice moves `mov (N\|M0) rX.o<1>:ud rY.o<1;1,0>:ud` | **297 total** (0.64 per message) | the landing pads that assemble a DPAS operand from the messages |
| uniform offset adds `add (1\|M0) rX, rY, imm` | **564 total** (≈1.2 per message, SLM+UGM) | one per message address |
| movs within ±2 instructions of a send | 275 | same population |

⇒ the staging pipeline (464 messages + 297 slice movs + ≈450–564 offset adds)
is **≈ 1.2 k of 4 754 instructions ≈ 25 %** of the kernel, and it is exactly the
population that the "interleaved mov" question is about (§3).

## 3. Interleaved `mov` — located

`mov` 1687 (35.5 %), by execution width and type:

| slice | count | verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| 32-lane `mov (32\|M0)` | 529 | mix of half→float promotions and fragment copies |
| 8-lane `mov (8\|M0)` | 444 | **the SLM landing pads** (297 of them match the `:ud ← :ud` slice pattern; the rest are half-precision lane plumbing) |
| 1-lane `mov (1\|M0)` | 267 | scalar/descriptor/push arithmetic |
| 16-lane `mov (16\|M0)` | 205 | uniform arithmetic |
| dst type `hf` / `f` | 394 / 354 | the FP16 line: the round-trip half↔float at every accumulator→half conversion |
| same-type (`:d←:d`, `:ud←:ud`, …) | 958 (56.8 %) | of which 297 are the SLM landing pads |
| type-changing | 714 (42.3 %) | `OpFConvert` is 74 sites; the rest are deliberate half↔float/half↔int promotions |
| immediates | 82 (39 zero) | constant materialisation |

Conclusion: there is **no free `mov` pool**.  The 714 type-changing moves are the
FP16 rounding line (contract §3 forbids touching them) and the 297 slice moves are
the *consequence* of the 464 SLM messages — one cannot be deleted without the
other.  This is why the "interleaved mov" direction, taken alone (delete movs,
keep the messages), has no legal candidate; the only way to delete those movs is
to delete the transport they serve.

## 4. DPAS, barriers, effective lanes, scratch

* **DPAS**: `dpas.8x8` 64 per warp, 128 per WG, 8.39 M per dispatch; every one is
  8×8×16 fp16→fp32 and every one is issued by a full 32-lane subgroup (100 % lane
  utilisation inside the DPAS).  No DPAS is duplicated: the 6 K-loops are distinct
  k-slices except §5.1.
* **Barriers / spills of the pipe**: `sync.bar` 12, `sync.allrd` 16, `sync.allwr`
  12, `fence.slm.none.group` 11, `sync.nop` 353 (scoreboard padding, 7.4 % of the
  kernel — a direct cost of the message stream: 353 nops for 640 messages ≈ 0.55
  nops per message).
* **Effective lanes**: grid (169,97) = 16 393 WGs, of which 168·96 = 16 128 cover
  the image (**98.4 %**); the 265 leftover WGs are fully out of bounds and still
  execute all 64 DPAS.  Each WG covers 64 pixels ⇒ the full dispatch launches
  16 393 × 64 = **1 049 152** pixel slots (the number n02b's address enumeration
  used) of which the in-bounds WGs cover 1 032 192 for 921 600 pixels
  (**89.3 %** useful).  The pixel tail (stage 11) runs on `sg<2` only: 2 of 8
  subgroups = **25 % lane efficiency for 420 instructions (8.8 %)**.
* **Scratch 0**, workgroup 24 576 B, push 128 B (plan row says 336 B incl. the
  constant block), `max_r = 127` ⇒ **the register file is already full; there is
  no register headroom for any M-batching variant**.

## 5. What each candidate class is worth

### 5.1 Weight reuse (F05 M-batch shape) — **rejected before coding**

Stage 1 loads `b0/b1` from `wb` inside the round loop and they do not depend on
`round`: the **same 4 weight fragments are loaded twice** (round 0 and round 1).
That is the *only* weight duplication in the kernel (stage 3: 16 distinct k/n
tiles, stage 4: 12 distinct Q/K/V tiles, stage 6: 8 distinct K tiles, stage 8: 8
distinct V tiles, stage 9: 4 distinct O tiles — no repeats).

* worth: **4 messages of 747 (0.5 %)** = 524 k messages per dispatch;
* cost: the F05 M-batch form must keep both rounds' accumulators live at once —
  4 more `MAT_C` 8×16 fp32 = **+16 GRF/lane** on a kernel at `max_r = 127`;
* verdict: rejected on the campaign's own budget rule (L9; n05c/n05d).  A variant
  that hoists only `b0/b1` (2 fragments = 4 GRF) still adds 4 GRF to the hottest
  block for 0.5 % of the message stream; not defensible.

### 5.2 Input duplicate read — **the intra-subgroup round trip is the only legal member**

All 464 fragment messages are one of two kinds:

| kind | transfers | messages | removable from source? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **cross-SG** transports (stage1 M → stage3 A, stage4 K/V → stage6/8, stage9 O → tail) | 48 | 384 | **no** — the writer SG ≠ the reader SG (`M` rows 8·(sg/4+4r) vs 8·sg; K rows 0..63 read by every SG), so a barrier + SLM is the only channel |
| **intra-SG** round trips (Q: stage4→6, Y: stage3→9) | **8** | **64** | **yes** — writer and reader are the same subgroup on the same rows (`8*sg`), so the fragment never has to leave the register file |

The Q round trip is a *pure transport*: `MAT_H` (fp16, 8×16, accumulator use) is
written to `shArena[0..2047]` and read back as `MAT_A` (fp16, 8×16, A use).  The
two SPIR-V types differ **only in the `MatrixUse` operand** (verified:
`build/spv-check.json → H_vs_A_type.same_except_use = true`), and the module
already performs this exact component-wise handoff **twice** (stage 8 `p_cache`
→ A, and stage 8→9 `ctx_half0/1` → A, the "S37-CONTEXT-A" path).  So the
transport is legal, precedented inside the same module, and adds no rounding point
(the values are half before and after).

* worth: **4 transfers = 32 messages (5.0 % of the 640 runtime messages)**, plus
  ≈ 2 slice movs + ≈ 16 offset adds + 8 `OpIMul` address ops per warp;
* budget: the retained value is 2 `MAT_H` = **4 GRF**, and it replaces the store
  payload (4 GRF) at stage 4 and the load destination (4 GRF) at stage 6, over a
  live range of *one barrier*;
* **this is the card's candidate.**

Y (stage3→9) is the same mechanism but the live range spans stages 4, 6 and 8 —
the three highest-pressure regions of a `max_r = 127` kernel.  Budget: +4 GRF
across the peak ⇒ rejected for this card (see `COST-LEDGER.json`, `RESUME.txt`
"next").

### 5.3 The 8-messages-per-fragment constant — the big share, out of reach this round

If a fragment transfer cost 2 messages instead of 8, the SLM stream would fall
from 464 to ≈116 (−75 % of 72.5 % of the messages, i.e. **−54 % of all messages**
and ≈ −15 % of the whole kernel).  That is the only lever in F20 worth more than a
few percent — and it is **not reachable from GLSL**: it requires the shared arrays
to be stored in the DPAS per-lane order, which the language exposes only through
`coopMatLoad/Store`; writing it by hand would replace the "same instruction on
both sides" mapping proof with an unverifiable layout assumption, and no offline
evidence chain (SPIR-V + FP multiset + NC parity) could prove it.  Recorded as the
direction that deserves F20's share, not as this card's change.

## 6. Bottom line

* F20 is an **issue/message consumer**, not a FLOP consumer: 84 M messages and
  623 M instructions per dispatch against 8.4 M DPAS.
* 66.5 % of the message slots are SLM, and 464 of them are cooperative-matrix
  fragment staging at a fixed cost of **8 messages + ≈0.6 mov + ≈1.2 add per
  transfer** — that is the 25 % staging tax, and it is the only large pool.
* Within it, the only *eliminable* work that does not change the owner map is the
  8 intra-subgroup transfers (Q, Y).  This card takes **Q** (4 transfers, 32
  messages, 5.0 % of the runtime message stream, ~1 % of instructions).
* The weight-dedup class is 0.5 % of messages and costs 16 GRF ⇒ rejected on
  budget; the interleaved movs are the staging's own landing pads ⇒ not
  independently removable; the 14.3 % debug-only messages are not issued and are
  excluded by contract.
