## Q1. Mapping — what does each (lane, component) mean, and why is the handoff the identity?

**Geometry of the Q plane.** Stage 4's Q step (`j = 0`, `region = n0/32 = 0`) owns
`m0 = 8*sg`, columns `{0..15}` (fragment `half0`) and `{16..31}` (`half1`).
Stage 6 wants exactly the same rows: `score_q0 = shArena[(8*sg)*32 + 0]`,
`score_q1 = shArena[(8*sg)*32 + 16]`, both `LROW`, stride 32.

| object | logical index | owner | consumers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `half0` (stage 4, region 0) | token `t = 8*sg + r`, channel `c = 0..15` | SG `sg` only | stage 6, `ki=0` A operand, `j = 0` and `j = 1` |
| `half1` (stage 4, region 0) | token `t = 8*sg + r`, channel `c = 16..31` | SG `sg` only | stage 6, `ki=1` A operand, `j = 0` and `j = 1` |
| `score_q0` / `score_q1` (stage 6) | same rows, same column halves | SG `sg` | 4 `OpCooperativeMatrixMulAddKHR` A operands |

Coverage: 8 SGs × 8 rows = 64 tokens, 2 fragments × 16 columns = 32 channels ⇒
**64 × 32 = 2048 halfs = the whole Q tile, each (t, c) written exactly once and
read exactly once per (j, ki) consumer**.  No token, no channel and no lane is
dropped, and no element is written twice (the capture is guarded by `region == 0`,
which is true for exactly one of the three loop iterations — `j = 2 − step`).

**Element map.** The parent's round trip is the identity on the logical index by
construction: `coopMatStore(half0, shArena, base, /*stride*/32, LROW)` writes
element `(r, c)` to `shArena[base + r*32 + c]`, and
`coopMatLoad(score_q0, shArena, base, 32, LROW)` reads element `(r, c)` from the
same address.  The candidate replaces that with `score_q0[d] = half0[d]`, i.e. it
claims `MAT_H[d]` and `MAT_A[d]` denote the same `(r, c)`.  That claim is not an
assumption invented here:

1. the two SPIR-V types are identical except for `MatrixUse`
   (`build/spv-check.json → H_vs_A_type`: component type 6 = f16, scope 3 =
   Subgroup, rows 8, cols 16, use 2 vs 0, `same_except_use = true`);
2. **the baseline module already performs this exact component-wise H→A handoff
   twice** — stage 8 (`MAT_A pa; pa[d] = p_cache[j][d]`) and, decisively, the
   stage 8 → 9 context handoff (`a[0..3] = ctx_half0[0..3]`, the "S37-CONTEXT-A"
   path at source lines 553-558), which is in the accepted baseline and passes the
   fixed-frame gold;
3. the same accessor (`fragment[d]`, `d = 0..3`) is used on both sides of the new
   handoff, so the candidate cannot introduce a *different* map — it either
   matches (as at stage 9) or it would break stage 9 as well.

Residual risk (stated, not hidden): if the B580 layout of a f16 8×16 accumulator
differed from the f16 8×16 A layout, the *baseline* stage-9 handoff would already
be wrong.  The mapping argument therefore inherits the baseline's own numerics,
which the gates have already accepted.

**Tail block.** The Q handoff has no tail: the 64 rows × 32 channels of Q are
covered exactly, the loop trip count is the constant 3, and the pixel boundary
(`px < 1280 && py < 720`, `oob` in stage 0) is untouched — the candidate does not
change any index computation of the pixel or token space (the only index
expressions removed are the two `(sg*8)*32` shared addresses; `build/spv-check.json
→ removed_loads` shows both addresses as `((sg*8)*32)` and `+16`).

## Q2. What real work is deleted (normal execution path only)

| # | deleted item | count per warp | basis |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | `send.slm` fragment load × 8 for `score_q0` | 8 | `load.slm.d32x8t` 304 → 288, address `(sg*8)*32`, RowMajor/stride 32 (`removed_loads`) |
| 2 | `send.slm` fragment load × 8 for `score_q1` | 8 | same, address `+16` |
| 3 | the 16 messages of the two Q-plane stores **at runtime** | 16 | the stores stay in the module but behind the uniform `debug` branch (`p0.z = 0`) |
| 4 | uniform offset adds for those 16 messages | ≈ 16 | `add (1|M0)` addressing each message (`COST-BREAKDOWN.md` §2.2) |
| 5 | register-slice moves for those messages | ≈ 10–16 | 297 slice movs over 464 messages |
| 6 | `OpIMul` × 8 + `OpIAdd` × 2 of the two shared addresses | 10 | `opcode_delta`: `OpIMul` 147 → 139, `OpIAdd` 252 → 250 |
| 7 | one WAR hazard (stage 3's M read vs the Q-plane rewrite) | 1 | see Q3 |

**Not** counted (contract §4 Q2): the 96 + 11 debug-only messages, the 6 debug
barriers and the debug element dumps.  They are not issued at `debug = 0` and
their removal is not a mechanism this card claims.  Neither is any
compiler-eliminated source: every item above is present in the captured ISA of
the parent.

## Q3. Overlap — producers, consumers, hazards

| value | producer | first consumer | coverer (WAR/WAW) | hazard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `acc0/acc1` (Q, fp32) | stage 4 Q DPAS (step 2) | `MAT_H(acc0/acc1)` conversion | the same variables in the next dispatch | RAW, register-local |
| `half0/half1` (Q, fp16) | stage 4 conversion + `full_norm` | **parent**: Q-plane store → stage 6 load; **candidate**: stage 6 A operand | parent: stage 6's load, then the M region of the next workgroup; candidate: stage 6's MulAdd | parent RAW through SLM (barrier `sync.bar` #9); candidate RAW in registers |
| shArena `[0..2047]` | stage 4 Q store (dead after the barrier) | stage 6 Q load (parent) | stage 1 of the *next* workgroup writes M over `[0..8191]` | parent has a WAR (stage 3 read of M rows 0..15 → Q overwrite) ordered by the stage-3→4 barrier; **the candidate deletes the write, so that WAR disappears** |
| K/V planes `[2048..6143]` | stage 4 K/V stores | stage 6 K (cross-SG), stage 8 V (cross-SG) | next workgroup | unchanged — the barrier stays for these |
| `p_cache` (P) | stage 6 | stage 8 (same SG) | — | unchanged (already register-only) |
| `ctx_half0/1` (C) | stage 8 | stage 9 (same SG) | — | unchanged (already register-only, the precedent) |

The barrier after stage 4 is **kept**: it is required for K and V (their readers
are other subgroups).  Deleting the Q round trip removes work *and* removes a
hazard, it does not serialise anything new.  Independent work that used to overlap
(stage-1 M stores vs stage-3 A loads, cross-SG) is untouched.

## Q4. New cost (everything that was added)

| item | count | comment |
|---|---:|---|
| register pressure | **+0 net** (2 `MAT_H` = 4 GRF kept live over one barrier; the store payload 4 GRF at stage 4 and the load destination 4 GRF at stage 6 are gone) | peak unchanged in kind; the fragment variables already exist in the module (no new local) |
| new uniform control flow | 1 `OpIEqual` + 1 selection (`region == 0`) and 1 `OpINotEqual` + 1 `OpLogicalOr` + 1 selection (`debug \|\| region != 0`) | both operands are uniform; after IGC's unrolling the `region` tests are compile-time constants |
| new moves | ≤ 8 `mov` per warp (the 8 component transfers, if the allocator cannot coalesce them; the stage-9 precedent is expected to coalesce) | worst case +8 against −26…−48 removed |
| repeated weight/A reads | **0** | no load added anywhere |
| address arithmetic | **−10** SPIR-V ops | two shared addresses disappear |
| shuffles / SLM round trips / barriers | **0 added** | — |
| effective lanes | unchanged | the capture runs on all 32 lanes of every SG |
| workgroups / grid / occupancy | unchanged | 16 393 WGs, local 256 |
| live-value peak | +4 GRF over one barrier (the Q fragment), −4 GRF at the stage-4 tail (store payload) | net ≈ 0 at `max_r = 127` |
| code size | SPIR-V +364 B (16 924 → 17 015 words +91); the ISA is expected to *shrink* by 20–40 instructions | the SPIR-V growth is glslang lowering `fragment[d] = …` as access-chain/load/store pairs, which IGC promotes to registers |
| scratch | 0 required; **if IGC spills, the card fails** (triage B, stop condition 2) | no Private object in the module either side |

Logic bytes vs DRAM bytes: the change moves **0 bytes** of DRAM traffic.  The
fragment bytes are SLM-only: 2 stores + 2 loads of 8×16 f16 = 1 024 B per warp
(134 MB per dispatch) that no longer cross the SLM port.

## Q5. Does it land in native? (prediction + falsification)

See `NATIVE-PREDICTION.md` for the full list.  The two decisive observables:

1. `load.slm.d32x8t.a32` **304 → 288** in the captured ISA of pipe 38 (a −5.3 %
   change in a class of 304; not a schedule artefact);
2. the two Q-plane `store.slm.d32x8t` move inside a **new uniform predicate** — in
   the ISA the Q publication becomes `(W&~f0.0) jmpi`-guarded, so the fragment
   stores issue only when `debug != 0`.

If both are absent ⇒ **A-class (implementation did not land)**, not "the mechanism
is worthless".  If they land but `scratch > 0` or `max_r > 127` ⇒ B-class.

## Q6. Is the whole thing faster? (what to measure, with which arms, how to attribute)

I cannot measure this (no GPU in this card).  The measurement this card asks for:

* **arm pair**: `base` = chain head `n09c-f09-b-share` plan (sha
  `25cd7faf…`), `cand` = this card's `plan.txt` (sha `a5555e6d…`), which differs
  on **one line** (ord 230) — so the delta is attributable to this PASS alone;
* **design**: ordinary `--pool --warm 1 --reps 30 --feedback`, two arms, 6
  alternating process groups (the campaign's `two-arm-6-alternating`), paired
  group means, two-sided t 95 % on 5 df; gates first (`fixed`, `fb2`), then
  `ordinary`; native + resources in the same batch;
* **expected magnitude**: at most **−5 % of the runtime message stream** of one
  dispatch, i.e. **≤ 0.07 ms** of the 1.367 ms, and ~1 % of its instructions.  It
  is a 1-PASS card with a small scope: the honest prior is a large CI relative to
  the effect, i.e. **D-class (INDISTINCT) unless the dispatch is message-bound**;
* **attribution rule**: only the native delta (Q5 1–2) may be credited to the
  mechanism.  If the messages drop and the time does not move, that is evidence
  about F20's bottleneck (issue slots are not the limiter, latency/DPAS overlap
  is), *not* evidence that removing staging work is worthless — the same
  mechanism over the sibling site (Y) would then be worth one more try only if
  the register budget allows it (it does not today: +4 GRF across stages 4/6/8 at
  `max_r = 127`).
