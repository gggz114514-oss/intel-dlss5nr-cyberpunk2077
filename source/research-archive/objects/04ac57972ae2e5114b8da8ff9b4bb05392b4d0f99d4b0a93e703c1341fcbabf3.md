## 2. Why the component transport should cost no messages

The candidate's 8 component stores are `OpAccessChain/OpLoad/OpStore` pairs on
*fragment variables* (glslang's lowering of `fragment[d] = …`, visible in
`build/spv-diff.txt`), not memory transactions.  IGC promotes Function-storage
objects to SSA registers (it already does so for the stage-9 context handoff,
which has the identical shape and produces **no** SLM traffic in the parent's
ISA).  Therefore: **no new `send` of any shape**.  If a `store.slm`/`load.slm`
pair appears around the Q capture, the transport was materialised in shared
memory ⇒ implementation error (not a valid arm), report it and stop.

## 3. Independent falsification: what "landed" does *not* prove

Landing (1)+(2) only proves that the two fragment loads are gone and that the
publication is guarded.  It does **not** prove that stage 6 receives the same
values; that is covered offline by
`build/spv-check.json` (structural diff: the only hunks in the module are the
capture, the guard and the removed loads; FP multiset 70/70 identical; NC 45/45
identical; `MatMulAdd` 16 → 16) plus the gate frames.  If the gates fail, the
first suspect is the `MAT_H[d] ↔ MAT_A[d]` map (Q1), and the fallback is the
explicit `coopMatStore`/`coopMatLoad` variant through a *new* packed plane (which
would keep the messages and is therefore not worth building).

## 4. If this lands but does not pay (B/C/D triage)

* **B** (side effects): look for `mov` > 1695 (transport not coalesced) or
  `max_r` / `scratch` movement; the fix is the explicit 4-component form of
  stage 9 (`a[0]=…;a[1]=…`, no loop) — already what the candidate does.
* **C/D** (no net win): the dispatch is not message-bound.  Do **not** extend to
  the Y round trip on the strength of this card alone — Y needs +4 GRF across
  stages 4/6/8 with `max_r = 127`, which is the n05c/n05d failure mode.  The next
  experiment in that case is a *sensitivity* probe, not another candidate: remove
  one full cross-SG transport (stage-9 O → tail) in a throwaway arm to measure
  what one fragment transfer is worth in milliseconds.  That number decides
  whether the remaining 48 cross-SG transfers deserve a redesign.
